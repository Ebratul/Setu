# Bangladesh Bank — import advance payment (summary)

This is Setu's own plain-language summary. It is a simplified summary for demo. Verify every figure with the official circular and your authorised dealer (AD) bank before you act. Setu is a simulated sandbox and does not move real money.

## What an advance payment is

An advance payment means the importer pays the overseas supplier before the goods are shipped or before they arrive. In Bangladesh, foreign exchange payments for imports go through an authorised dealer (AD) bank. An AD bank is a commercial bank that Bangladesh Bank allows to handle foreign exchange.

Advance payment carries risk for the importer. The supplier may not ship, or may ship the wrong goods. This is why the central bank sets limits and asks the importer to prove later that goods really arrived.

## The limit as stated in this summary

As given for this demo:

- An importer may generally pay an advance for imports up to **USD 20,000 per import** without a repayment guarantee from the supplier.
- This limit was raised from **USD 10,000** in September 2025.
- The rule is consolidated in the Foreign Exchange Policy Department (FEPD) import circular of **13 August 2026**.

The circular number is not given here. See the official circular. Limits and conditions can change, so check the current text.

## Above the limit

If the advance would be more than the limit, the importer generally needs another safeguard. Common options are:

- a guarantee from the supplier's bank that the advance will be repaid if goods are not shipped, or
- a different payment mode, such as a letter of credit (LC). An LC is a bank promise to pay the supplier once agreed documents are presented.

Your AD bank decides what it accepts. Ask the bank which route applies to your deal.

## Matching the advance with goods imported

After an advance, the importer must generally show that goods were actually imported. The proof is the bill of entry, the customs document for the imported goods. The importer matches the advance against the bill of entry through the AD bank within the period set by the rules.

If the goods do not arrive, the advance generally has to be brought back or settled according to the rules. Check the exact period and procedure with the AD bank. Missing the matching deadline can create compliance problems for the importer.

## Paying in CNY

Many Bangladeshi importers buy from China. Payment in Chinese yuan (CNY) is generally possible through CNY accounts and RTGS-type settlement channels at AD banks that offer them. Availability, charges and documents differ by bank. Check with your AD bank which channels it supports and whether the same advance limit applies in your case.

## How this relates to Setu

Setu's milestone escrow splits a supplier payment into tranches, for example deposit, pre-shipment and post-arrival. Money is released only after evidence checks pass. This means only a part of the order is paid before goods move.

Setu also uses USD 20,000 as a Tier 3 threshold. In Setu, any single release above USD 20,000 needs a one-time password (OTP) and a 10-minute cooling-off. This is a Setu control. It is similar in size to the advance limit but it is not the same rule. Setu's threshold is a product safety setting, not legal advice.

## What this means for an importer

- Keep each advance at or below the limit unless your bank approves another route.
- Plan for the bill of entry: keep the invoice, packing list and bill of lading ready.
- Ask your AD bank about the matching period for each advance.
- For larger orders, ask about an LC or a bank guarantee.
- Use staged payments so you do not pay everything before shipment.
- Treat this page as a starting point only. Verify with the official circular.
