# Incoterms basics for Bangladesh importers

An Incoterm is a short trade term in a sales contract. It says who arranges transport, who pays which costs, and where risk passes from seller to buyer. The examples below assume sea freight from China to Chittagong.

## Why the term matters for landed cost

Landed cost is the full cost of getting goods to your door: price, freight, insurance, duty, taxes and local charges. The Incoterm tells you which of these the supplier already included in the price. Comparing a FOB quote with a CIF quote without adjusting is a common mistake.

## EXW (Ex Works)

Under EXW, the seller makes the goods available at the seller's own premises. The buyer arranges and pays all transport, including pickup in China, export clearance and main sea freight. The buyer also pays insurance if wanted. Risk passes to the buyer when the goods are placed at the buyer's disposal at the seller's site. EXW is cheap on paper but gives the buyer the most work. It is less common for small importers.

## FOB (Free On Board)

Under FOB, the seller delivers the goods on board the vessel at the named Chinese port, for example Shanghai or Ningbo. The seller handles export clearance. The buyer arranges and pays main sea freight and insurance. Risk passes when the goods are on board. FOB is very common for Bangladesh importers because they can choose their freight forwarder.

## CFR (Cost and Freight)

Under CFR, the seller arranges and pays main sea freight to the destination port, for example Chittagong. The buyer pays insurance. Risk still passes when the goods are on board at the Chinese port, not at arrival. A buyer under CFR should consider buying cargo insurance.

## CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight)

Under CIF, the seller pays main sea freight and a minimum level of cargo insurance to the destination port. Risk passes when goods are on board in China, even though the seller pays freight and insurance. Many suppliers quote CIF Chittagong. The insurance is generally basic, so check its cover.

## DAP (Delivered at Place)

Under DAP, the seller arranges and pays transport to a named place in Bangladesh, such as a warehouse or port. The seller bears risk until the goods are ready for unloading at that place. The buyer handles import customs clearance, duty and taxes.

## DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)

Under DDP, the seller delivers cleared goods, with import duty and taxes paid, to the named place. The seller carries almost all cost and risk. In practice DDP into Bangladesh is hard for a foreign seller to do, so be careful with quotes that claim it.

## Comparison table

| Term | Main sea freight | Insurance | Risk passes to buyer | Import duty and clearance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EXW | Buyer | Buyer (if wanted) | At seller's premises | Buyer |
| FOB | Buyer | Buyer (if wanted) | On board at Chinese port | Buyer |
| CFR | Seller | Buyer (if wanted) | On board at Chinese port | Buyer |
| CIF | Seller | Seller (minimum cover) | On board at Chinese port | Buyer |
| DAP | Seller | Seller (bears risk) | At named place in Bangladesh | Buyer |
| DDP | Seller | Seller (bears risk) | At named place in Bangladesh | Seller |
